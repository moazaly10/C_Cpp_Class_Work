Problem 10.1 – Error Messages Produced by the Compiler

(a) After commenting out 'using namespace std;'
------------------------------------------------
Errors:
- ‘string’ was not declared in this scope.
- ‘cout’ and ‘endl’ were not declared in this scope.

Explanation:
The identifiers 'string', 'cout', and 'endl' belong to the std namespace.
By commenting out 'using namespace std;', the compiler cannot find them.
Solution:
Prefix these names with 'std::' (for example: std::cout, std::string).

(b) After removing 'Critter::' prefix
-------------------------------------
Error:
- ‘setHunger’ was not declared in this scope.

Explanation:
The compiler no longer recognizes 'setHunger' as a member of the Critter class.
The scope resolution operator 'Critter::' tells the compiler that this function
belongs to the Critter class. Without it, the definition becomes a standalone
function unrelated to the class.

Alternative Solution:
Keep 'Critter::' for all member function definitions or define the method
inside the class declaration directly.

Conclusion:
Both issues are related to missing scope resolution — one for namespaces (std)
and one for class membership (Critter).
